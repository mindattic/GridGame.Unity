**Status: CANON.** The owner adopted it on 2026-11-21, and it replaces every earlier GridGame premise: "Under Morrow", "A War in Heaven" and the colour/moon outline. Every story text (crawls, conversations, lore, quests, barks, glossary) must agree with it. **Amended 2026-11-21 (GG-T577, owner):** the Manalith is not a moon; the life in people is called **mana**; the Hierarch is the villain; and the story stays simple, because it is a video game. **Amended 2026-11-27 (GG-T1305, owner):** the player is **the Commander**, a person of Halcyon brought back from the dead, who leads but does not fight; every hero is a present-day person; the Warden is no longer the lead and no one in the story is a sleeper.

**In one line:** an ancient thing hangs in the sky beside the Moon. Long ago it was stopped and left there, orbiting the world, and now it is coming closer. Everyone calls it **the Manalith**. Its **mana** is also the life in every person. The Choir keeps it asleep with **the Lullaby**, a rite that takes people's mana to quiet it and keeps a share for the Choir's leader, **the Hierarch**. **The Commander**, a person of Halcyon who died the night it fell, is brought back 1,200 years later. The Commander does not fight; present-day heroes come to them for advice and leadership, because Halcyon's machines, the Mana Orbs among them, wake only near one of Halcyon's own. No one, the Commander included, holds all the answers. The tone is bittersweet, and the player chooses the ending.

**The sky.** There is one Moon, and there is the Manalith:
- **The Moon** is the ordinary one: round, pale and familiar, always the same size in the sky. Nothing in the story happens to it.
- **The Manalith is not a moon.** It is an ancient thing that was stopped on its way in and left hanging in the sky, where it orbits the world. It is a lumpy, potato-shaped mass of crusted, grown-looking stone, clustered blade-like crystals and bubbled deep blue and violet lumps flushed with red, with a faint light seeping out between them. Beside the constant Moon it is visibly larger in every chapter, because it is coming closer. The player measures time running out by how much of the sky it fills.

**The name is the clue.** *Manalith* is an old word built from *mana* and *lithos* (stone): "the mana stone". Everyone takes it as a plain name for where mana comes from. It is more literal than they know: something alive sleeps inside that stone.
