**The Somnolith** has hung asleep in the sky for ten thousand years. Now it is coming closer: anyone who looks up can see it larger than their grandparents saw it, while the Moon stays exactly the same size. This entry covers why.

1. **The arrival is resuming.** The Somnolith was stopped mid-arrival, not turned away. Every century of drawing lets the old descent continue a little further. No one knows what it will do when it completes.
2. **The draw never stopped.** Halcyon fell, but the Underspire still runs its neon on Halcyon's old taps, rewired by people who don't know or don't care what they are pulling on.
3. **The Choir keeps more than it needs.** The Lullaby takes more aether than the Somnolith's sleep requires, and the surplus goes to the Hierarch in the Cathedral. A starved sleeper is a restless sleeper, so every year the Lullaby has to take more.
4. **The shell is breaking.** As the Somnolith comes closer it sheds its crust. The Shardfall is where the first great pieces came down, and some of its curved spikes are not rock but the Somnolith's own body showing through.
5. **Its aether is flowing home.** This is **the blanking**. People near the lines, under a close Somnolith, or tithed too hard have their aether drawn back up into it, and their colour and self with it. What is left is white: porcelain shells, white-sheeted crowds, blank figures. The world calls them **the Hollow**. It is not hunger or malice, only water finding its level.
6. **Where its substance lands, things grow.** The Bloom is where pieces of the Somnolith fell and took root: red life in a white ruin, the first sign that what is coming is not only death.

**The sign in the sky.** The Moon is the fixed measure, always the same size. The Somnolith beside it swells chapter by chapter, its crystals and lumps plainer to the naked eye, until in Morrow's Shadow it fills the sky.

**The visual law.** White means hollowed and red means alive. Heroes wear the loudest colours in the game because they are still fighting for their own. Red bleeding through white, as in the porcelain knights' cracks and the Bloom's blossoms on white stone, means some aether is still inside.
