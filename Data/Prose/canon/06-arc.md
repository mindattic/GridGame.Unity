The campaign follows the Manalith's approach, from the Commander's return to the final choice. The Moon is drawn the same size over every region, the fixed measure. The Manalith beside it is drawn larger and closer in each region as the chapters go on, smallest over the Tithe Moors and largest over Morrow's Shadow, where it fills the sky.

## The Commander's arc

The Commander wakes in the Still with a body 1,200 years out of its time and a memory in pieces: how a door opens, how an orb is held, the faces of other commanders, but not why Halcyon fell or what the Manalith is. The road gives that back one piece at a time, and every piece is someone else's to tell. The Commander wakes the tech that shows each truth (a mural lit, an Ark door opened, an archive stirred) but does not remember it; a present-day hero or a Camp keeper reads it out. What the Commander learns, chapter by chapter, is what Halcyon did, and that they were part of it.

The same thing that makes the Commander the party's strength makes them hunted. Halcyon keyed its machines to its own people's mana, so near the Commander the orbs fight, the doors open and the Anchors answer. To the Choir that makes the Commander a stolen key: first a runaway with the caravan, then, once the Choir learns what came out of the Still, the one living person who could open or close the taps. Every claimant after the Hierarch wants that key too, which is why the final choice is the Commander's to give and the player's to make.

## Endings

Because the mana is shared, every ending costs everyone:

- **Keep it asleep.** Keep the Lullaby, without the skim: the Manalith sleeps and the world keeps its mana, paid for in people.
- **Cut the lines.** Close every tap and the pull stops. The Manalith drifts back out. People's mana dims to what it was before the Manalith came, so lives become shorter, quieter and plainer, but wholly their own.
- **Let it come.** Stop the Lullaby and let the ten-thousand-year arrival finish, without knowing what it will bring. What follows must stay true to the mystery and must not reveal what the Manalith is for.

## Decisions (owner, 2026-11-21)

- **The lead: the Commander** *(GG-T1305, owner 2026-11-27, replacing the Warden, the Last Sleeper, GG-T533/GG-T577).* The player is the Commander, a person of Halcyon who died in the Still the night Halcyon fell and was brought back 1,200 years later by the Still itself, when the tithe ran through its tanks. Not a fighter and not a unit: heroes come to the Commander for advice and leadership. Halcyon keyed its machines to its own people's mana, and present-day mana carries only the Lullaby's thinned thread, so Halcyon tech wakes only near the Commander: the Mana Orbs the party fights with, old doors, an archive for a moment (GG-ONE-LIGHT, Halcyon's key). That is why capable present-day people follow them, and why the party can field orbs at all.
- **No one holds all the answers or ancient powers.** The Commander's memory is in pieces: they know how parts of Halcyon worked, not why it fell or what the Manalith is. Every other character is a present-day person looking back at a fallen world that has forgotten its own history; what is known is spread across the cast.
- **Naming the Commander.** Story text says "the Commander", with the article and the role capitalised, like the heroes ("The Commander" at the start of a sentence). The player's profile name is a token a writer may use in direct address only. Text about the Commander is gender-neutral; the Commander's look is the player's pick of a female or male portrait, like every hero.
- **Every hero is a present-day person** of the world as it is now. No one in the story is a sleeper. *(GG-T577, GG-T1305.)*
