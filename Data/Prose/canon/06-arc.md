The campaign follows the Manalith's approach, from the Commander's return to the final choice. The Moon is drawn the same size over every region, the fixed measure. The Manalith beside it is drawn larger and closer in each region as the chapters go on, smallest over the Tithe Moors and largest over Morrow's Shadow, where it fills the sky.

## Endings

Because the mana is shared, every ending costs everyone:

- **Keep it asleep.** Keep the Lullaby, without the skim: the Manalith sleeps and the world keeps its mana, paid for in people.
- **Cut the lines.** Close every tap and the pull stops. The Manalith drifts back out. People's mana dims to what it was before the Manalith came, so lives become shorter, quieter and plainer, but wholly their own.
- **Let it come.** Stop the Lullaby and let the ten-thousand-year arrival finish, without knowing what it will bring. What follows must stay true to the mystery and must not reveal what the Manalith is for.

## Decisions (owner, 2026-11-21)

- **The lead: the Commander** *(GG-T1305, owner 2026-11-27, replacing the Warden, the Last Sleeper, GG-T533/GG-T577).* The player is the Commander, a person of Halcyon who died in the Still the night Halcyon fell and was brought back 1,200 years later by the Still itself, when the tithe ran through its tanks. Not a fighter and not a unit: heroes come to the Commander for advice and leadership. Halcyon keyed its machines to its own people's mana, and present-day mana carries only the Lullaby's thinned thread, so Halcyon tech wakes only near the Commander: the Mana Orbs the party fights with, old doors, an archive for a moment (GG-ONE-LIGHT, Halcyon's key). That is why capable present-day people follow them, and why the party can field orbs at all.
- **No one holds all the answers or ancient powers.** The Commander's memory is in pieces: they know how parts of Halcyon worked, not why it fell or what the Manalith is. Every other character is a present-day person looking back at a fallen world that has forgotten its own history; what is known is spread across the cast.
- **Naming the Commander.** Story text says "the Commander", with the article and the role capitalised, like the heroes ("The Commander" at the start of a sentence). The player's profile name is a token a writer may use in direct address only. Text about the Commander is gender-neutral; the Commander's look is the player's pick of a female or male portrait, like every hero.
- **Every hero is a present-day person** of the world as it is now. No one in the story is a sleeper. *(GG-T577, GG-T1305.)*
- **Open decisions (GG-T1305), each with a default, awaiting owner** (the owner confirms them in the OWNER canon task):
  1. **The `warden` class stays** as an ordinary present-day recruitable Tank, its kit unchanged; its lore is rewritten in the Warden-as-present-day-hero task. *(default, awaiting owner)*
  2. **"Manalift" is read as the Manalith** in the owner's brief. *(default, awaiting owner)*
  3. **The Commander has a female and a male portrait,** picked by the player. *(default, awaiting owner)*
  4. **In battle the Commander gives advice and a few orders,** never fights or takes a tile; the spec is the battle feature's. *(default, awaiting owner)*
  5. **The Warden's present-day faction is the Lantern Road,** keepers of the lanterns on the pilgrim road. *(default, awaiting owner)*
- **Every hero is one single character, named by role with the article:** "The Cleric" at the start of a sentence and "the Cleric" anywhere else, the role capitalised, never bare: the Warden, the Paladin, the Thief. Ids and assets stay `cleric`, `warden`. There are no personal names for heroes. *(GG-T565, replacing the earlier bare-name rule.)*
- **The villain is the Hierarch,** always with the article: the Choir's human leader, who alone knows the whole truth and drinks the skim. *(GG-T577.)*
- **The Manalith is not a moon.** An ancient thing, stopped long ago, hanging in the sky and orbiting the world, now coming closer. The world has one Moon, ordinary and always the same size. "Morrow" is not a name for the Manalith; the word survives only in the region Morrow's Shadow ("morrow" = tomorrow, where tomorrow's shadow falls). *(GG-T575, amended GG-T577.)*
- **The word is mana,** never essence. *(GG-T577.)*
- **Every class comes in a female and a male version,** and the player picks one when a hero joins, and the Commander has both portraits too. Story and mechanics are identical for both, so story text never assumes a hero's gender; heroes are referred to by role, and any pronouns follow the player's pick through the game's own tokens.
- **Tone: bittersweet.** Real losses and no clean wins, but warmth and hope between the heroes.
- **Endings: the player chooses** between keeping it asleep, cutting the lines and letting it come. The postgame regions reflect the choice.
- **Keep it simple** (owner, GG-T577: "its a video game so dont make it too complex"). No lore the game does not need.
- **Retired** (GG-T577), not to be used in any text: the Pale Concord, the Bright Host and its war, celestials and angels, the Hierarch as a celestial deserter, the Herald as a messenger of the Host, "Morrow" or "the moon" for the Manalith, "essence", heroes as sleepers, the Summon Circle as thawing sleepers. Also retired (GG-T1305): the Warden, the Last Sleeper, as the lead or protagonist; the rule that the Warden is the only sleeper; anyone in the story as a sleeper. The Echo hero class is cut (GG-T531).
