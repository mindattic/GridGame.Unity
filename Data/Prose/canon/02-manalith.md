**The Manalith** is an ancient thing that hangs in the sky beside the Moon. It is not a moon. Long ago it was stopped on its way in and left there, orbiting the world, and now it is coming closer. It is alive. This entry covers what it is and how it came to be in the sky.

## The core truth

- **It is not a moon.** The world has one Moon, round and pale, that has always been there and never changes. The Manalith is a living thing that came in from the dark about ten thousand years ago. It is never called a moon, by the text or by anyone in the world.
- **It was stopped.** The civilization that became Halcyon sang it to sleep in the last moment before it arrived and held it in the sky. It has orbited the world ever since, asleep.
- **Its shell is debris.** Over ten millennia it turned and slept while dust, ice and stray stone settled on it, layer on layer, the way a mineral crust grows, in lumps and clusters.
- **What it looks like.** An irregular, potato-shaped body, never a sphere. Its surface is grown rather than cratered: clusters of flat, blade-like crystals, bubbled deep-blue-to-violet lumps, flushed red in places, like a kidney stone or a desert rose, glints, and pale fibrous tufts like frost or hair. A faint light leaks out of the gaps between the crystals, brightest when it stirs. It looks organic and mineral at once, and it looks wrong.
- **It drags a wake.** As it turns in its orbit it sheds what has settled on it, and a long wake of debris trails across the sky behind it: crystal shards, lumps of crust, ice and dust, densest close to the body and thinning out to specks. The closer it comes, the heavier the wake.
- **Mana Dust is the Manalith breaking up.** Its debris burns and grinds apart high in the atmosphere, and the finest of it settles over the whole world as **Mana Dust**: a blue sparkle hanging in the air everywhere, raw mana that dissipates if nothing holds it. The breaking-up is visible across the campaign: the closer the Manalith comes, region by region, the thicker the sparkle.
- **The Manafall.** Now and then the wake lets go and Mana Dust comes down heavily, as slow, glinting blue motes out of the sky. People call it the Manafall. It comes more often the closer the Manalith draws.
- **Mana Shards are mana made stable.** Mana Dust won't keep; it drifts apart into nothing. Refined by the Manasmith into **Mana Shards**, it becomes a crystal that holds its mana instead of dissipating, which is why Shards are worth so much.
- **Mana Orbs are Halcyon's batteries.** Halcyon built vessels to capture Mana Dust and hold it ready: the **Mana Orbs**, scavenged now from its ruins. A fighter's orbs drink in dust during a battle and spend it on magic, but they can't hold it for long; whatever charge is left when the fighting stops settles back into dust, which the party keeps. How many orbs a fighter carries is how much mana they can bring to bear at once.
- **It makes magic possible.** Magic is mana dust gathered, concentrated and channelled into a phenomenon or a change in a living body: lightning, fire, ice, sleep, poison. All of it is mana that came from the Manalith, so without the Manalith there would be no magic at all. That is what its presence makes possible, not what it is for; the story still never says that.
- **It is coming closer.** The Moon is always the same size in the sky. The Manalith is not: generation by generation, and now season by season, it fills more of the sky. That is how anyone can see it is coming.
- **Its name is a description.** "The Manalith" ("the mana stone", from *mana* + *lithos*) sounds like a plain name and is more literal than anyone knows: a sleeper wrapped in stone. It is always "the Manalith" in full, never "Morrow", never shortened or nicknamed.
- **It is a force of nature, not a character.** It never speaks, bargains or explains itself. It sleeps, stirs, draws and sheds the way a storm or a tide does. Every motive the player sees belongs to people.
- **No one knows what it is for, and the story never says.** It never finished arriving, so no one has seen what it does when it does. The Choir calls it Mother and God; Halcyon's engineers called it a resource; the Bloom-touched call it a gardener; the Hollow are taken as proof that it is a predator. Every one of these is a guess, and no text may settle the question.
- **Closing line:** *"We always called it a stone that sleeps. It was never a stone. It was a sleeper, and we left it in the sky."*

## The history

1. **The arrival (about 10,000 years ago).** The Manalith came down toward the world. No one alive then knew why, and no one has learned since.
2. **The stopping.** Halcyon's ancestors stopped it in the last moment before landfall. Thousands sang the first Lullaby at once and held the Manalith asleep in the sky. Whatever it came to do never happened. It was only paused.
3. **The feeding.** Halcyon learned to draw on the sleeping Manalith's mana and built a civilization on it: the towers, the Anchors, the Still and the Arks. Over millennia the mana ran so deep in people that it became part of them. Whatever the Manalith is, a part of it now lives in everyone.
4. **The fall: the Undertow (1,200 years ago).** The Manalith's pull was growing, and Halcyon tried to escape it in the Arks. Lifting them took more mana than had ever been drawn at once, and the spike broke the Manalith's sleep for a single night. The mana surged back up every line; most of Halcyon was blanked by dawn; the tides rose higher than towers and threw the Arks inland; the Anchors cracked, and the city fell. That night the Warden, Halcyon's last guard, was asleep in the Still.
5. **The forgetting.** A remnant kept singing through the night and put the Manalith back under. They became the Choir. Halcyon's archives ran on mana, so when the city went dark its memory went dark with it. Over twelve centuries the truth became myth, then nothing. The world now believes the Manalith has always been there.

## The forgotten clue

Every painting, carving and star chart older than about ten thousand years shows the Moon alone. In a single layer of the record the Manalith is suddenly everywhere beside it, drawn as a lumpy, bristling shape next to the Moon's clean circle, and from that layer on every sky has both. The party finds this as relics along the road. The world has forgotten it, and the Choir does not want it remembered.
