## The campaign arc, in the game's chapter order

The Somnolith is drawn larger in each region's backdrops as the chapters go on. The story follows that approach.

| Chapter | Region | Discovery |
|---|---|---|
| Prologue | The Tithe Moors | The heroes wake from the Still into a world paying the Choir a tithe of people. |
| 1 | The Lantern Road | A moonless mural in a shrine raises the first question: why does the oldest art have no moon? |
| 2 | The Arkgrave | Under the wrecks is a harbour with no tide line. Halcyon tried to flee, and something made the seas rise. |
| 3 | The Anchor Wastes | The arches are taps, not supports. The draw is the pull. |
| 4 | Halcyon | A dead archive, woken briefly by the heroes' own light, shows the freezing, the feeding and the Undertow. Halcyon bound the Somnolith through its own people, and the light in everyone is the Somnolith's. |
| 5 | The Bloom | The Somnolith's life takes root here. It is not only a monster. |
| 6 | The Underspire | The active drain: someone is still pulling. |
| 7 | The Shardfall | The shell breaks and the body shows through. The arrival is resuming, and the blanking is its light flowing home. |
| 8 | Morrow's Shadow | Under the Somnolith, gravity fails and stone rises. There is no one to reason with, only the weight of it. |

- **Threaded locations:** the Still and the Choir's Cathedral run through the arc. The Still is the heroes' origin. The Cathedral is the Choir's seat and the heart of the Lullaby, where its surplus is revealed.
- **Postgame:** the postgame regions (Free Holds, Drowned Arks, Deep Shardfall, Under the Anchors) return to the world after the ending and must agree with the ending chosen.

## Endings

Because the light is shared, every ending costs everyone:

- **Keep the Lullaby.** Side with the Choir: the Somnolith sleeps and the world keeps its light, paid for in people. You may end the Choir's skim, which slows the decline, or leave it, and the sleep keeps thinning.
- **Cut the lines.** Sever every tap and the pull stops. The Somnolith drifts back up. Without the circuit, people's borrowed light dims to what it was before the seam, so lives become shorter, quieter and plainer, but wholly their own. The world learns to live under an empty sky, as before.
- **Let it arrive.** Stop the Lullaby and let the ten-thousand-year arrival finish. The Somnolith is something small, old and starved, caught mid-landing, whose light has been living in strangers ever since. No one knows if it is predator, protector or neither, and nothing in the game answers that before the choice. This is the gamble ending: people choose to stop holding it back without knowing what they are letting in. What follows must stay true to the mystery and must not reveal a tidy answer about what it is.

## Decisions (owner, 2026-11-21)

- **The lead: the First Sleeper, Warden.** One fixed protagonist of the Warden class, a class of its own (GG-T533) separate from Knight, wakes first in the prologue and thaws the others. The story has Warden's face and voice, and the summoned roster gathers around them.
  - **Who:** Halcyon's last guard, left on watch over the Still on the night of the Undertow.
  - **What they wake to:** they wake 1,200 years late, still on duty, sworn to a city that no longer exists, guarding sleepers in a world that has forgotten why.
  - **Their arc:** what a guard owes when the thing they guarded was the wrong thing to keep.
  - **Name:** just "Warden", with no article. Their own name was lost with Halcyon's dark archives.
- **Every character is an archetype.** A hero's role is their name, used bare like a proper name: "Warden", "Paladin", "Thief", never "the Paladin". There are no personal names.
- **Every class comes in a female and a male version** (the owner will generate the male counterparts). Either version may turn up. When a hero joins, through recruiting or a summon, the player picks which version they get, and that includes Warden.
  - The story and mechanics are identical for both, so story text never assumes a hero's gender.
  - Heroes are referred to by role; any pronouns follow the player's pick, through the game's own tokens.
  - The aim is equal footing, not another phone game full of pin-ups.
- **Tone: bittersweet.** Real losses and no clean wins, but warmth and hope between the heroes. Every ending costs something worth paying.
- **Endings: the player chooses.** A final choice between Keep the Lullaby, Cut the lines and Let it arrive. The postgame regions reflect whichever was picked.
- **The Choir's secret: only the Hierarch knows.** The Choir's rank and file are sincere believers. Only its leader, the Hierarch, knows everything: the moon is alive, the light in people is its light, and the Lullaby skims a surplus. The Hierarch is a human antagonist with a reason.
- **Echo is cut.** The Echo hero class is removed (GG-T531). Encore moved to the Tactician and Mirror Skin to the Courier. The blank silhouette was only a placeholder, not a story device.
- **The final region keeps its name, Morrow's Shadow:** "morrow" means tomorrow, and it is where tomorrow's shadow falls.
